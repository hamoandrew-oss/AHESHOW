Contracts.
How to use it.

The Contracts section is not a replacement for CEMAR or Sypro. It is the radar and the preparation room: GRIDLINE already knows the programme, the parts, the snags and the minutes, so it spots what may need a contract event, helps you prepare it with the evidence attached, and tracks the deadline once you have posted it wherever the client wants it. Money stays in each job's Commercial tab; the paper trail lives here; the two are joined.

1. The idea in one minute

Three kinds of event, chained together, numbered automatically:

EW-001 early warning→ INS-001 instruction→ CE-001 compensation event→ VO in the Commercial tab

You spot a risk and raise an early warning. The client instructs a change with an instruction. The instruction costs money or time, so it becomes a compensation event you price, they assess, and you agree. Once agreed, one tap puts it into the job's variation register so your next application for payment includes it. Every step is dated, linked to the last, and written to the audit trail.

Where the formal notice actually gets posted depends on the contract: the client's CEMAR, their Sypro, an email — or GRIDLINE itself for your own subcontracts. GRIDLINE prepares the wording and the evidence pack, you post it in thirty seconds, and you record the reference back here so the deadline is tracked where you actually look every morning.

Everything in Contracts belongs to a job. A contract is created inside a job; its events sit on that contract; the dashboard and inbox read across every job at once.

2. Set up a contract, say where it lives

  1. Open CONTRACTS in the sidebar, then the SET-UP tab.
  2. Press + CONTRACT, pick the job, then MAIN CONTRACT (you and the client) or SUBCONTRACT (you and a subcontractor).
  3. Choose where it is administered: CEMAR, SYPRO, EMAIL or GRIDLINE. Main contracts default to CEMAR, subcontracts to GRIDLINE. This one setting decides what the paperwork section on every event says.
  4. A main contract reads its client and value live from the job's Commercial tab — change the sum there and it changes here. Add the reference — for example NEC4 ECC Option A — dates, project manager and commercial manager. It saves as you type.
  5. Set the status to ACTIVE. Events can now be raised on it.
One main contract per job is normal. Add a subcontract for each subcontractor you want to administer formally — events on a subcontract can be linked to events on the main contract without anything being copied between them.

3. The morning routine: Radar

Open Contracts and stay on the RADAR tab. Read it top to bottom:

  • Contract health per job — a percentage that explains its own deductions (section 14).
  • The six counters — open early warnings, open CEs, outstanding instructions, overdue, not yet posted (events you have raised but not yet put into CEMAR or Sypro), and radar findings.
  • Commercial exposure — what's potential, notified, quoted, assessed, agreed and implemented, kept separate so a risk estimate never looks like agreed money. Tap any of the largest CEs to open it.
  • Needs you — every open item ranked by deadline, money at stake, risk and status, grouped Overdue / Today / Next 7 days. OPEN, ASSIGN, DONE on each.
  • Radar found — things GRIDLINE noticed in the job's own records that may need a contract event but have none: parts delayed from a supplier, a task well past its finish, a meeting minute that reads like an instruction ("please proceed", "revise the detail"), a certificate short of the application, a proposed variation unanswered for a fortnight, a site record open for weeks. No AI involved — it is always on. RAISE EW / RAISE CE / RAISE INS creates the event with the evidence already linked; DISMISS silences that finding.
  • Copilot — the deeper AI pass, when you ask for it.

The main GRIDLINE dashboard's Today list also carries your nearest contract deadlines and any agreed money not yet in payment, so you don't have to open Contracts to be reminded.

The contract brief

At the top of the RADAR tab, WRITE BRIEF gives you a short AI-written read across every contract: which position needs you today and why, then up to six contract actions, most urgent first, with the references, clause numbers and days left taken from your own records. It leads on the clocks and treats anything not yet posted as urgent, because a notice that hasn't reached the client's system protects nothing. It never invents a deadline. It is separate from the dashboard's morning brief on purpose: that one is about the site, this one is about entitlement.

4. Early warnings

Raise one the moment you think something could increase cost, delay completion or affect the work — the contract rewards early notice and punishes silence.

  1. On the EARLY WARNINGS tab press + EARLY WARNING and pick the contract. It's numbered EW-001, EW-002… within that contract.
  2. Give it a title and description. On a phone, 🎙 VOICE dictates straight into the description.
  3. Set the risk (LOW MEDIUM HIGH), the potential cost, the programme impact, the owner and a due date for the response.
  4. Record what was agreed at the risk-reduction meeting in ACTIONS AGREED.
  5. Move the status along the ladder: DRAFT OPEN UNDER REVIEW ACTION REQUIRED CLOSED.

From an early warning you can press RAISE INSTRUCTION FROM THIS or RAISE COMPENSATION EVENT — the new event arrives already linked.

5. Instructions

An instruction records what the project manager told you to do — a changed detail, a stopped activity, a new requirement. Record who issued it, who it was issued to, and the date. Status runs ISSUED → ACTIONED → CLOSED. An instruction outstanding for more than a week counts against contract health.

If the instruction changes the price or the programme, press RAISE COMPENSATION EVENT on it. The CE carries the instruction's description and links across.

The two NEC4 clocks

On contracts set to the NEC4 form (in SET-UP), GRIDLINE works out two periods itself and counts them down on the CE, in the radar, and on the dashboard's Today list.

ClauseWhat runs outRuns fromStops when
61.3Notifying a compensation event: eight weeks from becoming aware of it. Miss it and the Prices, the Completion Date and Key Dates are not changed — unless the event arose from the Project Manager's or Supervisor's own instruction, notification, certificate or changed decision.The aware dateYou enter the date it was notified, or the CE moves on from Potential
62.6Replying to a quotation: two weeks, or a longer period agreed under 62.5. If there is no reply, the quoting party can notify the failure, and if it continues for two more weeks the quotation is treated as accepted. GRIDLINE tracks both stages.The quotation dateA reply is recorded

Which way round. The same clause is a risk or an opportunity depending on your side. On a main contract you are the Contractor: 61.3 is your exposure, and the client's silence on your quotation is your opportunity — GRIDLINE tells you when notifying them would start deemed acceptance. On a subcontract you administer, you are the Project Manager: a quotation you sit on can become accepted against you.

Assumed dates. CEs raised before the clocks existed were given an aware date copied from the event date and marked assumed. They show greyed and raise no deadline until you confirm the real date. A time bar worked out from a date GRIDLINE made up would be worse than none.

The clocks are arithmetic from the dates you enter and the published periods. They don't read your contract — amendments, Z clauses and agreed extensions can change the real period. Check it against your contract; serving the notice on time stays with you.

7. Pricing a CE

Stop opening Excel. Every CE has a cost build-up:

  1. In the + add line… menu pick a category — labour, plant, materials, subcontractors, temporary works, design, preliminaries, delay, risk or other.
  2. Type a description, quantity, unit and rate. The line total and the grand total calculate as you go.
  3. In EVIDENCE on each line, note where the number comes from — the timesheet week, the hire ticket, the supplier quote reference. It appears in the evidence list automatically.
  4. Press USE AS QUOTATION to copy the total into the quoted value.
  5. Below the lines, the strip shows estimate → quotation → assessment → agreed → actual with the variance between each stage. Fill the actual cost in later to see what the job really made on it.
  6. EXPORT CSV gives the whole build-up as a spreadsheet for anyone who insists.

8. The paperwork: prep for CEMAR, or issue here

The last section of every event page has two boxes that look different on purpose.

Internal workspace (amber) is yours: strategy, your real view of the cost, negotiation position, management comments. It never leaves GRIDLINE and is never part of what you issue.

The blue box changes with the contract's administered-in setting.

Contract administered in CEMAR, Sypro or email

  1. Write the wording in the draft box — or DRAFT WITH COPILOT and edit the result. COPY WORDING puts it on the clipboard for pasting into CEMAR.
  2. Press READY FOR CEMAR — BUILD EVIDENCE PACK. One file downloads: the event, the wording, the cost build-up, every linked record, and every photo and drawing embedded, on your letterhead. It prints to PDF from the browser. Attach it in CEMAR.
  3. Back in GRIDLINE, type the CEMAR reference, the date you posted it, and the response date CEMAR gave you. From then on the deadline lives in the radar, and the event shows IN CEMAR. Until you do, the register shows it as NOT IN CEMAR and the radar counts it under "not yet posted".

Contract administered in GRIDLINE (your own subcontracts)

  1. Write the formal wording in the draft box — or press DRAFT WITH COPILOT to have it drafted from the event's own fields and linked evidence. Read it. Edit it. It's a draft, not a decision.
  2. Press ISSUE FORMALLY and confirm. The text is locked with its version number, date and device.
  3. Need to correct an issued notice? Draft again and issue as v2. Version 1 stays on the record — corrections supersede, they never overwrite.
  4. For a paper or PDF copy, print the event page from the browser.
Nothing is issued automatically — not by the Copilot, not by the radar, not by a status change. The evidence pack is a preparation, not a notice; only you post it, and only the confirm on ISSUE FORMALLY creates a formal record here.

9. Evidence and links

A CE that can't show its evidence is an argument. Every event can carry and link:

  • Attachments — photos and PDFs straight from the phone, viewable in place.
  • Programme — link the affected activities; the event shows their dates and how late they are, and you set programme impact to none, potential or confirmed with a number of days.
  • Drawings — link sheets from the job's drawing folders.
  • Site records — link snags; their photos become evidence too.
  • Other contracts — link a main-contract CE to the subcontract instruction it caused. If a main-contract event has subcontracts on the job and no downstream link, a warning reminds you to consider one. Linking copies nothing between contracts.

SHOW SUPPORTING EVIDENCE gathers everything across the whole chain — the early warning's photos, the instruction's PDF, the CE's cost evidence, the linked site records and their pictures, the comments — into one list. That is your bundle if it ever goes further than the site cabin.

The event timeline on each page draws the chain in date order — site record, early warning, formal issue, instruction, CE, quotation, agreement, payment — and every item is a link.

10. Getting agreed money paid

This is the step legacy systems lose. When a CE reaches ACCEPTED with an agreed value, the page shows a red banner until you press INCLUDE IN PAYMENT. That creates an instructed variation in the job's Commercial tab with the agreed value and a back-link, so the adjusted contract sum, OWED and AT RISK, and your next application all pick it up. Agreed-but-not-in-payment money is flagged on the dashboard, in the inbox and on the Today list until it's done.

11. The three tabs and the four-part page

The section has three tabs, and they map onto three jobs: RADAR is what needs you; EVENTS is one register of every early warning, instruction and CE, with type chips, open/closed, search and + RAISE; SET-UP is the contracts themselves, which you touch once per job.

Every event page — EW, instruction or CE alike — has the same four parts, so you learn the layout once:

12. Contract Copilot

On the dashboard, press SCAN THIS JOB. The Copilot reads the job's own records — open snags, meeting minutes, open actions, delayed parts, late tasks — and lists potential contract events you may not have raised: an early warning nobody wrote, an instruction given informally in a meeting, a delay that may be a CE. Each one says why, cites its evidence and gives a confidence level.

Your choices: REVIEW the reasoning, RAISE EARLY WARNING or CREATE CE (the evidence links come across), LINK EXISTING EVENT if it's already covered, or DISMISS. It suggests; you decide. It cannot issue anything.

The Copilot and drafting need an Anthropic API key on the hosted app — KEY ▸ API in the sidebar. The key stays in your browser.

13. From site: snag to early warning

You don't need the Contracts screen to start a contract event. On any open snag in a job that has a contract, press → EW. A draft early warning is created on the main contract with the snag's description, its photos linked as evidence, and the original capture date preserved on the record. Complete and issue it later from a desk. The snag shows EW LINKED so nobody raises it twice.

14. Contract health, explained

Health starts at 100 and only moves for reasons it shows you:

DeductionWhy it matters
Overdue responsesMissed contractual deadlines lose entitlement
Open high-risk early warningsUnmanaged risk to cost or programme
High-value unresolved CEs (over £25k or 10% of the contract)Big money still in dispute
Programme-critical eventsConfirmed impact on completion
Instructions outstanding over 7 daysWork instructed but not actioned
Agreed CEs not in paymentMoney you've won but haven't applied for

85% and above reads Healthy; 65–84% Attention required; below that, At risk. Fix the listed items and the number follows — there's nothing else in it.

15. Who did what — audit and access

Every change to a contract event — raised, status moved, value changed, comment added, attachment added, formally issued, included in payment, Copilot suggestion accepted or dismissed — is written to the activity log in Settings with the date, time, device and role. Name each device in Settings so the log is worth reading. Formal records are never edited in place.

In Site Mode the section is visible but money is hidden: site staff can raise early warnings, read instructions and complete their own items, and never see CE values or the internal workspace figures. PM mode sees everything. Fuller named roles arrive with team accounts.

16. Plain-English glossary

TermMeaning here
Early warning (EW)A notice that something could cost money, delay the job or affect the work. Raise early; the contract expects it.
Instruction (INS)A direction from the project manager to change, stop or add something.
Compensation event (CE)An event the contract says is the client's risk — you're entitled to time and money for it, once notified, quoted and assessed.
QuotationYour price and programme effect for a CE.
AssessmentThe project manager's view of what the CE is worth, if they don't accept your quotation.
ImplementedThe agreed CE is in the contract sum and being paid.
Main contract / subcontractUpstream (you and the client) / downstream (you and your subcontractor). Events on each can be linked without sharing content.
Internal workspaceYour private notes on an event. Never issued.
Formal contract recordWhat was actually issued, locked by version.
ExposureMoney that may be coming or going but isn't agreed yet. Kept separate from agreed change.
